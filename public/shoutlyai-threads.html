The Threads problem

Most businesses treat Threads like a shorter Instagram. That’s the mistake.

Threads is a text-first conversation platform. Polished branded graphics, promotional captions, and hashtag stacks that work on Instagram actively hurt you here. Threads rewards opinion, personality, and dialogue — things most business accounts don’t know how to produce consistently.

🖼️
Posting branded graphics with promotional captions
Threads users scroll past polished branded content instantly. The platform was built for conversational text — the graphic-heavy approach that works on Instagram gets zero engagement here. Text-first posts with personality outperform image posts 4:1 on Threads.
#
Using hashtags like Instagram
Threads hashtags have almost no algorithmic impact. The platform distributes content based on engagement signals — replies, reposts, likes — not tag matching. Adding 20 hashtags makes posts look spammy and reduces credibility without any reach benefit whatsoever.
📳
No distinct brand voice for Threads
LinkedIn voice (professional, story-driven) and Instagram voice (visual, hashtag-heavy) both fail on Threads. Threads requires a third voice — casual, direct, opinionated, conversational. Most businesses don’t have this defined, so they sound like nobody.
🔗
Not using thread chains for longer content
A single 500-character post can’t tell a full story. Thread chains — a sequence of connected posts that each earn the scroll — are the format that builds the deepest audience on the platform. Most businesses don’t know how to structure one.
💥
Posting announcements instead of conversations
Threads audiences respond to questions, takes, and opinions — not announcements. “We just launched X” gets ignored. “Why does X still not exist in India?” gets 200 replies. The platform rewards posts that invite a response, not ones that just inform.
🕛
Posting inconsistently because Threads feels low-priority
Most businesses treat Threads as experimental. But the platform rewards early, consistent presence. Businesses posting 5+ times a week now are building an audience that compounds as the platform grows from 150M to 500M+ users over the next 2 years.

Chains of 3–6 posts are ideal — long enough to tell a complete story, short enough to avoid dropout.
