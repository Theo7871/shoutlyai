The LinkedIn problem

Most businesses post on LinkedIn like it’s Instagram. It doesn’t work.

LinkedIn rewards different content than every other platform — personal insight over polished graphics, short paragraphs over long blocks, contrarian takes over congratulatory posts. Most businesses get this wrong and get ignored.

🖼️
Posting promotional content that no one engages with
LinkedIn’s algorithm heavily suppresses purely promotional posts. “Check out our new service” gets 8 impressions. A story about the client problem your service solved gets 8,000. Most businesses don’t know this distinction and keep posting the wrong thing.
📝
Writing walls of text that nobody reads on mobile
80% of LinkedIn is accessed on mobile. A single paragraph of 5 sentences appears as an unreadable block. Posts that get 10,000+ impressions use one sentence per line, strategic white space, and a hook in the very first line before the “see more” truncation.
🕛
Posting too rarely to build a following
LinkedIn creators who post 4–5 times a week grow 10x faster than those who post once or twice. But writing 5 story-driven, insight-led posts per week is genuinely hard when you’re running a business. Most businesses fall to once every few weeks, which the algorithm ignores.
😅
Using the wrong hashtag count and type
Instagram needs 30 hashtags in a specific mix. LinkedIn recommends 3–5 maximum — highly specific, not broad. Using 20+ hashtags on LinkedIn actively flags your post as spam and reduces distribution. Using broad tags like #Business instead of #StartupIndia reaches no targeted audience.
👤
Not distinguishing between personal and company page content
Personal profile posts outperform company pages 5:1 on LinkedIn for organic reach. First-person founder stories drive comments, follows, and inbound leads in a way company updates never do. Most businesses only post on the company page and wonder why nobody sees it.
📈
No system for consistent content week after week
A great LinkedIn post on Monday, nothing for 3 weeks, then another post. That inconsistency kills follower growth. LinkedIn’s algorithm rewards accounts that post consistently — but writing professional, story-driven content every day without a system is what stops most people from even trying.
